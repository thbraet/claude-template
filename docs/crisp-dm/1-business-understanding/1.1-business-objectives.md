### Problem Area
- **Problem Domain:** Supply chain workforce planning at store level
- **Problem Description:** Colruyt stores (CLP format) have no reliable forecast of incoming deliveries (carts, pallets, boxes) per store section (food, vers, diepvries, non-food) until the day before, when transport planning finalizes. This forces 1,800 store planners (geranten) to schedule workforce based on gut feeling, last year's same-week data, and personal experience. The result is systematic over- or understaffing, directly impacting labor costs and operational efficiency.
- **Project Status:** New case (~90% certain to proceed). First phase (variability analysis) has started — 6 years of historical delivery data obtained from Guido Kuylit. Initial analysis tool built and demonstrated to Wim De Keyser.
- **Motivation:** Direct P&L impact from over/understaffing. Koen De Vos requires data-driven proof that delivery variability is significant before committing to a full forecasting solution. Strategic supply chain evolution (new CLP category structure: vers, kruidenierswaren, non-food, dranken) adds further complexity that manual planning cannot absorb.
- **Target Group:** Store managers (geranten) and workforce planners across CLP stores, who use Plato for daily/weekly scheduling.

### Current Solution
- **Current Approach:** Plato (internally developed workforce planning system, live since March 2020) drives all store workforce planning. Long-term planning is based on same-week-last-year results with trend corrections, finalized by April 1st for the full fiscal year. Short-term adjustments are made monthly (for the next month, 3-7 weeks ahead). Actual delivery volumes are only known the day before via transport planning. The old system also had a centrally managed "forecast" based on moving averages with heavy manual intervention.
- **Strengths:**
  - Plato is well-established with structured long/short-term workflow
  - Per-store driver customization (different task times per store type/size)
  - Dashboard with weekly/monthly KPIs (productivity, carts, hours, revenue)
  - Supports multiple planning horizons (annual, monthly, weekly, daily)
- **Weaknesses:**
  - No structural mid-range forecast (3-6 weeks ahead) for delivery volumes
  - ~13% average deviation between Plato prognosis and actual deliveries
  - 1,800 planners making subjective decisions with varying skill levels
  - No systematic updates between annual plan and day-before actuals
  - Feestweek/promotion impacts only partially captured
  - Category mapping from DC dragers to store sections is not straightforward
- **User Acceptance:** Mixed. Experienced geranten cope well with current system; less experienced ones struggle significantly. Interpretation differences between stores are common (e.g., confusing 8 carts vs. 28 carts has occurred).

## Business Objectives

### Primary Objective
Predict the number of incoming transport units (carts, pallets, boxes) per CLP store, per day, per store section (food, vers, diepvries, non-food), 3-6 weeks ahead, to enable more accurate workforce planning in Plato.

### Business Questions
1. Is there significant variability in daily store deliveries (per section, per driver type) that justifies building a forecasting model?
2. Can we forecast deliveries per driver per section with a MAPE below 10% (target: below 5%) at the 3-6 week horizon, outperforming the current same-week-last-year approach?
3. What is the mapping between DC transport units (F1, F2, F4, DIV, VERS, FRUIT, FRIGO, ...) and store sections in Plato (food, vers, diepvries, non-food)?
4. Which data sources (ASB, ODCF, sales forecast, transport planning) can serve as predictive signals at the required horizon?

### Constraints
- **Staffing:** No available Supply Chain data science capacity. Smart Data Team (Thibauld full-time, Joni half-time) must execute. Supply chain domain expertise must come from stakeholders (Kevin, Michael, Wim, Guido).
- **Budget:** Colruyt budgets on hold; financial results on April 1st determine investment room. No external tooling budget expected.
- **Data:** DC-to-store section mapping is complex (one transport unit can contain multiple assortments; no 1:1 mapping). Data discovery phase is substantial. Plato driver data is not structurally in the data warehouse.
- **Skepticism:** Koen De Vos requires proof of variability before greenlighting a full model. The variability analysis is a gate.
- **Timeline:** 2-month deadline (target completion: 2026-05-26).
- **Scope:** CLP stores only (OK stores excluded for now).

### Expected Benefits
- Standardized, data-driven workforce planning across all CLP stores
- Reduced subjectivity and inter-store inconsistency in scheduling
- Improved productivity KPIs through better staff-to-workload matching
- Reduced overtime costs (understaffing) and idle hours (overstaffing)
- Foundation for future integration with Plato short-term planning workflow
