- **Data Mining Maturity:** Experienced data science team familiar with forecasting and classification. This project serves as a methodology benchmark — applying the full CRISP-DM pipeline to a well-known dataset.

### Problem Area
- **Problem Domain:** Binary classification — historical passenger survival prediction
- **Problem Description:** The sinking of the RMS Titanic (April 15, 1912) resulted in 1,502 deaths out of 2,224 passengers and crew. Survival was not random — certain groups (women, children, upper-class passengers) had significantly higher survival rates. The challenge is to build a predictive model that identifies which passenger characteristics were most associated with survival.
- **Project Status:** New
- **Motivation:** Kaggle competition entry and CRISP-DM methodology exercise. The Titanic dataset is a canonical ML benchmark that allows end-to-end pipeline development in a well-understood domain.
- **Target Group:** Kaggle leaderboard submission; secondary audience is the data science team for methodology reference.

### Current Solution
- **Current Approach:** Gender-based baseline (`gender_submission.csv`) — assumes all and only female passengers survive.
- **Strengths:** Simple, interpretable, captures the strongest single predictor (sex)
- **Weaknesses:** Ignores all other features (class, age, family size, fare, embarkation port); expected accuracy ~76.5%
- **User Acceptance:** Baseline only — not a real solution

## Business Objectives

### Primary Objective
Build a binary classifier that predicts passenger survival (0 = deceased, 1 = survived) for 418 test-set passengers, maximizing prediction accuracy on the Kaggle leaderboard.

### Business Questions
1. What passenger characteristics (sex, class, age, family size, fare, embarkation port) are most predictive of survival?
2. Can a model significantly outperform the gender-only baseline (~76.5% accuracy)?
3. What is the achievable accuracy ceiling given the available features?
4. Which feature engineering techniques (family size, title extraction, cabin deck, fare binning) yield the largest accuracy gains?

### Constraints
- **Data:** Fixed dataset — 891 training samples, 418 test samples. No additional data collection possible.
- **Missing values:** Age (~20% missing), Cabin (~77% missing), Embarked (2 missing) require imputation strategies.
- **Compute:** Local machine; no GPU required for tabular data.
- **Timeline:** 2 weeks (target completion: 2026-04-13).
- **Tools:** Python, scikit-learn, pandas, matplotlib/seaborn. MLflow for experiment tracking.

### Expected Benefits
- End-to-end CRISP-DM methodology reference for the team
- Benchmarked ML pipeline (preprocessing, feature engineering, model selection, evaluation)
- Reusable code patterns for future classification projects
- Kaggle leaderboard score as an objective performance measure
